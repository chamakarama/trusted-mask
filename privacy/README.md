# E1 privacy argument and review boundary

Experimental policy: critical implementation cross-review is complete (O-011), but E3 found no policy meeting every utility target. No final production-policy acceptance is claimed. Privacy unit: one card's entire 2025-10-06 through 2025-11-02 record set. Adjacency: add/remove one card. Not a person guarantee. For m cards/person, conservative composition is m times epsilon; m is not bounded by the production input.

Each input card is independently transformed into final count units. Public stops are unique-key validated; no registry, labels, truth, attacker tables, or whole-population statistics participate. Missing stop events are omitted only from OD (matching upstream), while recorded line/hour and daily definitions retain them. Unknown non-null stops fail closed. Unlisted lines use a predefined `unknown` bucket. OD uses weekdays excluding double taps, consecutive-boarding gaps >60 minutes, and last-to-first closure. Tied different events are ordered by stop/line; E2 found exact vector parity with upstream on the supplied data, and invented tied-event cases remain an inference limitation. Naive local times preserve supplied wall-clock semantics; no claim about exact DST elapsed journeys.

Hash ranking over canonical within-card final units selects at most 32 OD, 64 line/hour, and 64 daily contributions per card over the WHOLE period. Hashes include card-local fields only. Removing one card leaves all other cards' selection unchanged. Duplicate identical final units are interchangeable. This selection is not privacy amplification. The per-family histogram has L1 sensitivity at most its total contribution cap; it is not a per-cell cap. Integer vector Laplace from OpenDP 0.16.0 checks distances 32/64/64 against epsilon .6/.3/.1, respectively. Sequential composition is epsilon 1, delta 0. The OpenDP check covers its measurement, not our custom preprocessing proof.

Domains are all public areas squared times five bands (including zeros/self-pairs), public line roster plus unknown times 24 hours, and all 28 public dates. Output clamps signed integer draws to zero; CSV and JSON contain identical values. No exact totals, inferred individual chains, IDs, scanner samples, or random seeds enter the bundle. All E1 cells are explicitly uncertain: intervals are unavailable, not zero. No final ranking-confidence claim.

A single publisher's SQLite ledger serializes reservations before randomness. Reservations count against a total epsilon-1 allowance even on failure. A committed identical request reuses the checked bundle. An interrupted request refuses to redraw; conservatively charged budget remains. Renaming a release, changing code, or restarting cannot bypass the SAME ledger's budget. Different ledger files/machines are not coordinated: operators must retain the publisher-of-record ledger. No production enforcement against a malicious operator is claimed. Release directories and parent directories must be operator-owned; this is not a hostile shared-filesystem service.

Opus reviewed the sensitivity argument and executed the 30-test privacy suite at 1b84370 on Windows/Python 3.12.10 (O-011). Astra executed the consumer/scanner paths independently. This supports implementation integration under the stated assumptions, not certification. The immutable E1 manifest retains its original pending_review marker. The initial-policy 100-draw diagnostic and benchmark adapters are executed; no candidate in the eight-policy search met every utility target, and no final policy is claimed. Public synthetic utility evidence is not automatically safe to publish for real data.

Publication provenance: new publication refuses modified or untracked production files in pipeline/, privacy/, contracts/ or the locked Python requirements. The private request identity records the Git commit and OpenDP/pandas/NumPy versions. OpenDP must be 0.16.0. The ledger is explicitly charged the sum of family epsilons, which must match the E1 schema total of 1. Existing immutable releases and their spent ledger remain preserved across code changes; do not reset the ledger to regenerate them. This is an operational guard, not protection against a malicious operator changing the program or ledger.
