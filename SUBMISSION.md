## Measured results (synthetic benchmark)

All values come from `evidence/evidence-summary.json` for release `e1-aura-001`, unless another file is named. That immutable release was published by publisher commit `5438e3c`; the evaluation revision that produced the summary is later. The attack curve also appears in `evidence/e4/trajectory.json`.

- **Why not share rows:** on a fixed group of 1,000 synthetic cards (stops within 500 m, times to the hour), **4 known (stop, hour) sightings single out 97.5%** of travel cards once names are removed (B0), 97.5% with fixed pseudonyms (B2), and 87.4% even with k ≥ 5 suppression (B2-k, of the 922 cards left). 2 sightings: 70.0%, 3 sightings: 92.6% (95% Wilson ranges in the app). The organisers' own sample gives 97.0% / 97.4% / 90.4%. RouteSafe releases no rows, so this lookup is **not applicable**. That is not a claim of 0% risk. Rotating pseudonyms with finer time rounding (MOB-S2's suggestion) were not tested.
- **Decision kept (weekday mornings only):** the protected weekday-morning top-3 route shortlist equals the exact one (3/3, regret 0). The exact same top 3 came back in **100 / 100** fresh noise draws of the same policy (`exact_top3_count`); the decision target (≥ 2/3 overlap, regret ≤ 10%) also passed 100/100. Other time bands and start-area filters were **not** tested; the app says so.
- **Noise ranges (no extra privacy cost):** 95% noise-only ranges from the public noise scales, same rule as `privacy/uncertainty.py` (`evidence/e6/uncertainty.json`). For one cell chosen in advance: ±161 trips per route, ±640 boardings per line-hour cell, ±1,918 taps per day. A shortlist is picked from the noisy numbers, and users filter after looking, so the planner uses one fixed family: ±571 for all 2,205 released route cells at once, around the signed value with endpoints clamped at zero (±483 holds for the prechosen 420 morning routes only). Routes below that level are flagged "could be pure noise", a conservative warning, not proof of zero (all night top-3 routes, e.g. 341 trips). Line-hour cells within ±640 of zero are grey ("could be zero").
- **Volume-weighted error (WAPE):** routes at area level **25.1%** (median 24.1% over 100 draws; **misses the team's 20% target in 100/100 draws**), line × hour 14.7%, daily 7.2%; town-level routes 14.2% (that figure applies to town totals only). Big cells dominate this measure.
- **Per-card cap:** it leaves out 12.1% of route trips (the trips of cards with more than 32), so route totals run low; single noisy cells can still read high (cap loss 5.6% for line-hour, 7.6% for daily).
- **Organisers' utility tasks:** native MOB-U1 0.00 (average error 56% over 1,163 route cells with 20+ trips), adapted MOB-U2 0.00, adapted MOB-U3 0.77. **MOB-S1 utility is not passing.** The row baselines score B0 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00, B2 0.85 / 1.00 / 1.00 and B2-k 0.00 / 0.40 / 0.79 (native tasks, label-informed field catalogue; `row_baseline_utility` in Astra's reviewed summary). Read utility and risk together: the high-utility rows are the ones that expose 97% of cards.
- **Policy search:** eight predeclared candidate policies; none met every target. No final policy is claimed.
- **Scanner:** see R1 above (development macro-F1 0.902 vs 0.646 for baseline B1, sensitive recall 0.916; all 25 sensitive misses flagged for a person's check; independent probe 0 wrongly safe).

- **Membership simulation (reviewed in issue #3):** three preselected synthetic card profiles, with all other records and the target profile known. AUC .603/.569/.592; detection rates 1.49%/1.52%/2.23% at measured false-positive rates .86%/.93%/1.02%. Separate calibration and test draws; target-wise Wilson intervals in `evidence/e7/membership.json`. This simulates neighbouring releases, not repeated attacks on the one artifact, and does not establish zero risk.

## Honest limits (say these in the video and Q&A)

- The privacy guarantee is per card, for this period and this one release, with the policy pending review. It is not person-level anonymity and not a certification. In plain words: take two versions of the data that differ only by one card; any result the release could show is at most e¹ ≈ 2.7× more likely under one than the other (two possible histories of the same card: 7.4×). For someone who knew all other records and was 50/50, that means at most 73%. It does not bound what data about related people might reveal.
- Destinations are inferred, and area-level counts are noisy (about 25% volume-weighted error; ±571 per route number for the noise alone). The per-card cap can reduce counts; after capping and noise, the ten busiest exact-ranked morning routes came out 8–18% below their exact inferred counts. The morning shortlist is what survives reliably; night and small-area routes may be pure noise.
- The checksum shows the files match their manifest. Without a trusted signature it does not prove who published them or how they were computed.
- The budget ledger is a local file: it guards against accidental re-runs, not against a dishonest publisher. Every further release spends more budget.
- "No timestamps" means no exact event timestamps: hour bands, clock hours and calendar dates are public dimensions of the release.
- The AI assistant only picks one of a few typed requests. It gets the typed question and the public area list, never operator records or release counts. It cannot issue private queries or invent unsupported breakdowns; the app's own code runs the approved computations over the released tables.
